The Verification Layer
That Unlocks the Next Trillion
Cr3dentials is an income verification company. We verify the revenue of real businesses, e-commerce merchants, gig workers, payment processors, and produce a tamper-proof record of that income that lenders and capital partners can rely on when making credit decisions. What makes us different is where that record lives: on a shared digital ledger accessible to institutional lenders globally, not locked inside a single bank's system. That single difference opens a $3.5 trillion private credit market to a class of borrower it has never been able to serve efficiently.
I. The Macro Thesis, Private Credit Meets Digital Infrastructure
Context Where Capital Is Moving

Private credit, lending outside of traditional banks to businesses and institutions, is a $3.5 trillion global market. Historically it has operated through bilateral relationships, slow manual processes, and geographic limitations. A lender in New York has no efficient way to evaluate and fund a merchant in Dubai or a driver network in Johannesburg. Deals are slow, expensive, and limited by who knows whom.

A new category of financial infrastructure is changing this. Shared digital ledgers allow verified asset data and loan terms to be recorded in a way that any credentialed participant globally can access and trust, without needing a shared bank or intermediary. The market for assets recorded this way has grown from $5 billion in 2022 to $24 billion by mid-2025, a 380% increase in three years. Standard Chartered projects this market reaching $30 trillion by 2034. BlackRock, Fidelity, Apollo, and JPMorgan are all actively building products in this space. Private credit has emerged as the largest single category within it.

Our Position The Step That Has to Happen First

Any physical or income-generating asset, before it can be recorded on a shared digital ledger and used as the basis for a loan, must first be verified in the real world. This step is not optional. It is the foundation. Without verified proof of the underlying income or asset, there is no collateral. Without collateral, there is no loan. The verification step is where the entire process either holds or breaks, and it is the step that most of the market has overlooked.

The conversation around digital credit infrastructure has focused on well-established asset types, government bonds, real estate, corporate debt, because those already have audit trails, legal structures, and institutional custodians. What has been largely ignored is a much larger and less served category: the operating income of real businesses. The e-commerce merchant doing $2M in annual sales. The gig economy driver with three years of consistent earnings. The payment processor moving millions through a regional market. These are real income streams. They are creditworthy. They simply have no standardized mechanism for a lender to verify and rely on them, until now.

Cr3dentials connects directly to the platforms where this income is generated, e-commerce platforms, ride-hailing apps, payment rails, verifies the income data privately, and produces a structured, tamper-proof record that capital partners can use as the basis for a credit decision. We are not a lender. We are the verification infrastructure that makes lending to this category of borrower possible at scale.

Revenue logic: Our fee is earned at the point of verification. As deal volume grows across our partner network, so does our revenue. We do not take credit risk. We provide the infrastructure that enables others to take it with confidence.
Competitive Angle From One Lender to a Global Market

Today, a merchant looking for growth capital typically has one option from their platform, a single lender at a fixed rate, on terms set entirely by that platform. There is no competition, no negotiation, and no alternative if the terms do not work. The merchant either takes what is offered or goes without.

Cr3dentials changes the structure of this dynamic. By producing a verified, standardized record of a merchant's income that any lender can evaluate, we make it possible for a broader pool of capital partners to assess and potentially fund that merchant. The merchant gains access to a wider range of potential lenders. Capital partners gain access to a verified pipeline of borrowers they previously had no efficient way to evaluate. Cr3dentials earns a fee on each verification event that enables that process.

We are starting with Shopify merchants as our initial profile because of the platform's scale and the accessibility of its data. The same verification infrastructure extends to any platform that holds business income data: inventory systems, payment processors, marketplace records. Each new data source we connect to expands the pool of borrowers we can verify without rebuilding the model.

Regulatory Tailwind A Policy Environment Moving in Our Direction

The current US administration has made the development of digital financial infrastructure a stated policy priority. In March 2025, the SEC and CFTC jointly issued the first clear regulatory classification of digital financial assets in over a decade, providing the legal certainty that institutional capital needs before committing to a new asset class. Capital that was waiting on the sidelines for that clarity is now beginning to deploy. Cr3dentials is positioned to provide the verification layer that makes that deployment possible for business income as an asset class.

A merchant whose revenue lives on an e-commerce platform does not fit cleanly into that model, so they are either declined or offered terms that do not reflect the actual strength of their business.

We verify that merchant's income at the source, producing a structured record that capital partners can rely on. We already have over 1,000 merchants matching this profile in active outreach, with deal flow building as the first loan batches are processed.

Funnel Rates How Our Numbers Compare to Industry Standards

Private credit and small business lending qualification rates are well documented. Large US banks approve only 13.5% of small business loan applications on average. Non-bank lenders reach 28.9%. The most common reasons businesses fail to qualify are weak cash flow documentation (40% of rejections), limited operating history (23%), and incomplete financials (19%).

Our outreach pre-screens for all three of these failure points before a business reaches a lender. We verify income at the source, meaning every business that enters our pipeline already has documented, verifiable revenue in a format a lender can act on. Our 7% qualify rate is a deliberately conservative starting assumption, industry data suggests a well-screened, pre-verified list of this type would typically qualify at 10-15%. We have modeled 7% to reflect the early-stage reality of building the pipeline from scratch.

The 7% close rate on qualified pipeline reflects the natural gap between a business expressing interest and a deal completing, rate discussion, documentation, timing. The 5% bear case accounts for slower early conversion before the origination rhythm is established. Both sit within the 5-15% range typical for private credit on qualified small business pipeline. The small business loan default rate dropped to 4.7% in 2025, reinforcing that well-underwritten business credit carries less risk than its reputation suggests.

Default Risk How the Lending Structure Protects Capital Partners

Droplinked is a commerce platform, not a direct lender. Their role is origination and merchant relationships. The capital side is handled by their lending partners, who operate under UCC Article 9 for the US market.

UCC Article 9 is the US commercial law framework governing secured lending. A lender operating under a perfected Article 9 security interest holds a legally recognized, first-priority claim on the borrower's assets, including inventory, accounts receivable, and business equipment. In the event of a default, that claim gives the lender legal standing to initiate claim and liquidation proceedings through a structured process. This is the same legal framework that institutional lenders and asset-backed credit facilities use. The verified income record we produce is the documentation layer that supports that claim, making the asset not just understood but legally actionable.

Founder Signal Why We Back This Partner

Droplinked's founder crossed $2 million in referrals to capital financiers in Q4 2025, UAE only, through traditional lending channels, with the support of a close working relationship with PayPal MENA for regional data access, but without the structured verification infrastructure we are now building together. They hold a $100M credit line in MENA and are entering the US as their primary next market. Droplinked is a commerce platform that connects merchants to capital, not the capital itself. Their growth is not constrained by a balance sheet. It is constrained by the size of the verified merchant network they can reach, which is exactly where we come in.

Further validation: Textile (Israel), Tribeca Capital (New York), and TradeVu (Nigeria) are each independently forming relationships with us across three separate geographies, confirming that demand for verified business credit infrastructure is a global pattern, not a regional one.

III. Kasi Money, South Africa Gig Economy
Market The Underrated Bet

South Africa's gig economy is valued at approximately $230 million in 2025 and growing. The more important context is the labor market driving it. South Africa's official unemployment rate fell to 31.4% in Q4 2025, the lowest reading since 2020 and the second consecutive quarterly decline, yet nearly 1 in 3 working-age South Africans still cannot find formal employment. The youth unemployment rate sits at 43.8%. At those levels, gig platforms are not an alternative to formal work. They are the primary source of income for millions of people, and verifying that income is not a nice-to-have. It is a financial necessity for anyone in that population seeking access to credit.

Kasi Money is a financial services platform serving this gig worker population. They grew from 55,000 to over 100,000 drivers in a single year, nearly doubling without a significant product change. That is the market growing around them. Uber accounts for approximately 40% of the driver base in their user profile, giving us an initial addressable pool of around 40,000 drivers as we move from pilot to full production. Our base case targets reaching 5,000 driver verifications per month, a 12.5% penetration rate of that accessible pool. As Kasi Money expands to additional local platforms, the pool grows with them.

Revenue projections here are intentionally conservative. The $600 monthly minimum fee provides a contractual floor while volume builds. Breakeven is 1,200 verifications per month at $0.50 each. Above that, every additional verification is incremental margin with no additional overhead.

V. Compliance, Licensing & Operational Risk
Risk Where We Are and What We Need

We are transparent about where we stand on compliance. Cr3dentials is built to the technical specifications of the key standards required to handle sensitive business data at an institutional level. What we do not yet hold are the formal certifications and local operating licenses required to close larger institutional contracts.

ISO 27001 is an internationally recognized information security certification. It is the minimum standard most enterprise and institutional partners require before sharing sensitive business data with a third-party provider. This is our first certification milestone.

SOC 2 Type II is an independent audit of our security, availability, and data handling practices. It is a standard requirement for US-based financial institutions and enterprise technology buyers. This follows ISO 27001 on our roadmap.

GDPR governs the handling of personal and business data across international markets. Our data architecture is built to these principles. Formal documentation and data processing agreements are in progress.

Local data processor licenses are required to operate as a data verification provider in South Africa under the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA), and in Nigeria under the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA). We are not yet registered in either jurisdiction and are pursuing both in parallel with our commercial operations. These registrations are a prerequisite for scaling enterprise-level contracts in each market.

Impact on deal size: The absence of formal certifications today creates a ceiling on the size and type of counterparties we can currently serve. Larger regulated institutions typically require ISO 27001 and SOC 2 as a baseline. Obtaining these certifications is expected to expand the range of counterparties and contract sizes accessible to us across all three markets.
