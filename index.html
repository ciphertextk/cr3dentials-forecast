The Verification Layer
That Unlocks the Next Trillion
We verify business income privately off-chain and place cryptographic proof on-chain, converting e-commerce merchants, gig workers, and payment processors into real-world assets that the global private credit market can fund over blockchain rails. Before any asset can be tokenized it must first be verified, that verification step is the infrastructure gap we fill. Underwriters and lenders looking for profitable, real-economy RWA cannot move capital on-chain without it. We are the bridge between the operating world and the onchain credit market, and we are building it at exactly the right moment.
I. The Macro Thesis, Onchain Private Credit & RWA
Context Where the World Is Going

The RWA tokenization market reached $24 billion in mid-2025 having grown 380% in three years. Standard Chartered projects $30 trillion by 2034. Over 200 active RWA projects are live with TVL hitting $65 billion in 2025. BlackRock, Fidelity, Apollo, and major banks are all launching tokenized credit products. Institutional capital is not asking whether to move onchain, it is asking what assets are worth funding when it gets there.

Private credit has emerged as the largest onchain RWA category. According to rwa.xyz, distributed onchain private credit stood at approximately $5 billion as of March 2026, against a traditional private credit market estimated at $3–3.5 trillion globally. The gap between those two numbers is the opportunity. The infrastructure closing that gap, verification, proof, underwriting rails, is what we are building.

Our Position Verification Before Tokenization

Any physical or income-generating asset, before it can be turned into a token and recognized as an RWA on-chain, must first be verified off-chain. This is not optional, it is the foundational step in the tokenization lifecycle. Without verified proof of the underlying asset, there is no collateral. Without collateral, there is no loan. Without a loan, there is no yield for the investor. The verification step is where the entire chain either holds or breaks.

The conversation in RWA has been dominated by Treasuries, real estate, and bonds, assets that already have well-understood legal structures and institutional custodians. The opportunity being missed is business income data: the Shopify store with $2M in GMV, the Uber driver with three years of consistent earnings, the Nigerian merchant clearing $50K a month through a crypto payment rail. These businesses generate real, recurring, verifiable income. They are real-world assets. They simply have no mechanism to prove it on-chain, until now.

Cr3dentials connects directly to the platforms where this income lives, Shopify, Uber, Stripe, PayPal, verifies it privately off-chain, and publishes cryptographic proof on-chain. That proof becomes the verification event that an underwriter needs before committing capital. We are not the lender. We are the infrastructure that makes lending possible for an entirely new asset class. Each deal processed through our partners is designed to begin with a verification event from us.

Revenue logic: Every dollar of private credit that moves on-chain through our partners requires a verification event. Volume drives our revenue. The macro trend drives volume. As more lenders enter the onchain private credit market, demand for our verification infrastructure grows with them, not just through our current partners, but through every originator who needs to prove the asset before they can fund it.
Competitive Angle From One Lender to a Global Market

Today, merchants looking for growth capital largely deal with a closed system, one platform lender at a fixed rate, with terms set by the platform and no competition. The merchant has no leverage, no alternatives, and no way to shop their verified revenue to a wider pool of investors who might fund them at better terms.

Cr3dentials changes the structure of this market. By verifying a merchant's income off-chain and placing proof on-chain, we convert their operating data into an asset that any lender in the global private credit market can evaluate. Those looking to deploy capital into verified business credit now have access to a structured pipeline of opportunities they could not previously reach. The merchant gains access to a broader pool of potential lenders. Cr3dentials earns a fee on each verification event that enables that process.

We are starting with Shopify as our ICP entry point, it offers the scale and data accessibility to build a verified pipeline quickly. But the verification infrastructure works across any platform that holds business income data: inventory systems, payment processors, marketplace sales records. The addressable merchant universe grows every time we add a new data source, without rebuilding the infrastructure from scratch.

Regulatory Tailwind The US Moment

The current US administration is actively positioning America as the global capital of crypto finance. The March 2025 SEC and CFTC joint guidance provided the first clear classification of digital assets in over a decade, removing the regulatory ambiguity that had held institutional capital on the sidelines. Capital that was waiting is now moving. For Cr3dentials, this is not a tailwind, it is a structural alignment. We are delivering verifiable, privacy-preserving business income data on-chain at the exact moment the regulatory framework is being built to receive it.

Funnel Rates How Our Numbers Compare to Industry Standards

Private credit and SME lending qualification rates are well documented. Large US banks approve only 13.5% of SME loan applications on average. Non-bank lenders reach 28.9%. The most common reasons businesses fail to qualify are weak cash flow (40% of rejections), limited operating history (23%), and poor financial documentation (19%).

Our funnel pre-screens for all three of these failure points before an application reaches the lender. We verify income directly from the merchant's platform, meaning every business that enters our pipeline already has documented, verifiable revenue in hand before a lender ever sees their file. This makes our 7% qualify rate a deliberately conservative assumption, we are not sending unscreened cold lists. We are presenting pre-verified income data to backers who already want to deploy capital into growing businesses. Industry data suggests well-targeted verified lists qualify at 10–15%. We have modeled 7% to reflect the early-stage reality of building pipeline from scratch.

The 7% close rate on qualified pipeline reflects natural friction even on well-matched deals, rate discussion, documentation, and the gap between interest and commitment. The 5% bear case accounts for slower conversion before Droplinked's US origination rhythm is fully established. Both sit within the 5–15% close rate typical for private credit on qualified SME pipeline. The SME loan default rate dropped to 4.7% in 2025, reinforcing that well-underwritten business credit carries less risk than its reputation implies.

Default Risk How the Lending Structure Protects Investors

Droplinked is an onchain commerce platform, not a direct lender. Their role is origination and merchant relationships. The capital side is handled by their lending partners, who are structured and compliant with UCC Article 9 perfection for the US market.

UCC Article 9 is the US commercial law framework governing secured transactions. A lending partner that has filed a perfected security interest under Article 9 holds a legally recognized, first-priority lien on the merchant's assets, including inventory, accounts receivable, and business equipment. In the event of a default, that perfected lien gives the lender the right to initiate claim and liquidation proceedings through a structured legal process. This is what separates structured private credit from informal lending, and it is why UCC Article 9 compliance is a baseline expectation among institutional credit investors. The onchain proof we provide is the documentation layer that supports that enforcement, making the asset not just verified but legally actionable.

Founder Signal Why We Back This Partner

That result was achieved with the support of a close working relationship with PayPal MENA, which provided data access in the region, but without the onchain verification infrastructure we are now building together. They hold a $100M credit line in MENA and are entering the US as their primary next market. Droplinked is an onchain commerce platform that connects merchants to capital, not the capital itself. That distinction matters, because it means their growth is not constrained by a balance sheet. It is constrained by the size of the merchant network they can reach and verify, which is exactly where we come in.

Parallel validation: Textile (Israel), Tribeca Capital (New York), and TradeVu (Nigeria) are all forming relationships with us independently, confirming that demand for verified onchain private credit is converging globally, not just in one market.

III. Kasi Money, South Africa Gig Economy
Market The Underrated Bet

South Africa's gig economy is valued at approximately $230 million in 2025 and growing. But the more important number for our projection is the labor market context that is driving people into gig work in the first place. South Africa's official unemployment rate fell to 31.4% in Q4 2025, the lowest reading since Q3 2020 and the second consecutive quarterly decline, yet nearly 1 in 3 working-age South Africans still cannot find formal employment. The youth unemployment rate sits at 43.8%. At those levels, gig platforms like Uber are not an alternative to formal work, they are the primary economic lifeline for millions of people.

This structural reality is what drives Kasi Money's growth and what makes our verification opportunity durable. Kasi Money grew from 55,000 to 100,000+ drivers in a single year, nearly doubling without a significant product change. That growth mirrors the underlying labor market: more people entering gig work as the formal sector fails to absorb the available workforce. When unemployment peaked at 33.2% in Q2 2025 before declining, the informal and gig economy absorbed much of the difference. Our verification product sits directly on top of this dynamic.

Uber captures approximately 40% of the driver base in Kasi's user profile, giving us an initial addressable market of ~40,000 drivers. Our base case targets reaching 5,000 drivers per month in verifications, a 12.5% penetration rate of the accessible Uber driver pool. Given that Kasi Money doubled their driver base in 2025 and is expanding to additional localized platforms, the total market we are penetrating grows alongside them. If their 2025 growth rate repeats in any form, the driver pool we target expands materially without us needing to acquire a new partner.

V. Compliance, Licensing & Operational Risk
Risk Where We Are and What We Need

We are transparent about where we stand on compliance. Cr3dentials is operationally aligned with the standards required to serve enterprise clients, our architecture and data handling practices are built to the specifications of ISO 27001, SOC 2, and GDPR. We are ready by the standards of those frameworks. What we do not yet hold are the formal certifications and local operating licenses required to close larger institutional deals.

Specifically, the path forward on compliance looks like this:

ISO 27001, information security management certification. This is the minimum threshold required by most enterprise and institutional partners before they will share sensitive business data with a third-party verification provider. We are targeting this as our first certification milestone.

SOC 2 Type II, service organization control audit covering security, availability, and confidentiality. Required by US-based financial institutions and enterprise SaaS buyers. This follows ISO 27001 in our roadmap.

GDPR Compliance, governing the handling of European and increasingly global user data. Our data architecture is designed with GDPR principles built in. Formal documentation and DPA agreements are in progress.

Local data processor licenses, operating in South Africa and Nigeria requires registration as a data processor under the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) in South Africa and the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) in Nigeria. We are not yet licensed in either jurisdiction. These registrations are a prerequisite for scaling enterprise-level verification deals in each market and are being pursued in parallel with our commercial operations.

Impact on revenue: The absence of formal certifications today creates an upper bound on the deal sizes and counterparty types we can currently access. Enterprise-grade financial institutions, regulated lenders, and large credit funds typically require ISO 27001 and SOC 2 as a prerequisite. Obtaining these certifications is expected to expand the counterparty types and deal sizes we can access across all three markets we currently operate in.
